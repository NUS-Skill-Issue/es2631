# Argument craft

A critique needs an answer to the question. Its thesis should state a judgement about the reasoning and the basis for that judgement. Avoid a generic claim that the proposal has both strengths and weaknesses.

## Build the argument

Start from the evidence and identify which findings most affect the conclusion. Give each paragraph a distinct contribution. When two paragraphs repeat the same observation under different standards, combine them or explain the difference in what each standard reveals.

Use enough context for the reader to understand the judgement. An introduction can be short. A standalone paragraph usually needs no separate introduction. Do not invent a hook, anecdote or dramatic statistic to make an argument more engaging.

Connect the evidence to the conclusion explicitly. For example, an unclear success criterion prevents evaluation of whether a trial met its goal. The significance comes from that consequence, not from the word “unclear” itself.

## Counterarguments

Present the strongest relevant alternative the evidence supports. Distinguish a documented opposing view from your own possible objection. Concede what is true and explain whether it changes, narrows or leaves intact the main judgement. Do not attribute a self interested motive without support.

A strong proposal may deserve a favourable critique. Do not automatically recommend another pilot or more research. Identify a specific next step only when it follows from an actual limitation or the user asks for recommendations.

## Organisation

Follow an order specified by the brief. Otherwise choose an order that develops the argument. There is no mandatory nine standard sequence in this package.

Conclusions should integrate the decisive findings and answer the question. Do not introduce new evidence or recap every paragraph by name. Match the confidence of the recommendation to the evidence.

## Reader check

Can a reader identify the judgement, locate its evidence and explain the inferential connection? Can they tell which facts come from the source and which interpretations are yours? Revise the parts where the answer is unclear.
