# Applying the Intellectual Standards

Use the standard to answer a concrete question about the reasoning. State the judgement and show its basis. The following prompts adapt the supplied starter's framework guidance. They are not a marking rubric.

| Standard | Decisive question | A useful critique move | Common confusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clarity | Can the reader understand the claim or concept? | Identify an ambiguous term and distinguish its possible meanings. | Adding detail before resolving the meaning. |
| Accuracy | Is the information true or adequately verified? | Check the source, measurement or corroboration behind a factual claim. | Calling an unsupported statement false. |
| Precision | Is the statement specific enough for this purpose? | Identify a missing unit, baseline, population, interval or condition. | Treating more decimal places as better evidence. |
| Relevance | Does the evidence help answer this question? | Explain the connection, or mismatch, between the measure and the goal. | Equating an interesting fact with useful support. |
| Depth | Does the reasoning address the problem's difficulties? | Trace a dependency, constraint or mechanism that affects feasibility. | Merely asking for more information. |
| Breadth | Are other pertinent perspectives or interpretations considered? | Test the argument through a materially different viewpoint. | Listing stakeholders without using their perspectives. |
| Logic | Does the conclusion follow from the premises? | Identify a missing inferential step or an inconsistent assumption. | Treating accurate premises as sufficient for any conclusion. |
| Significance | Does the reasoning focus on what matters most? | Explain which consideration changes the decision and why. | Calling an issue important without showing its consequence. |
| Fairness | Are interests and competing views treated even handedly? | Represent an objection fairly and examine whose costs are omitted. | Assuming equal weight is always fair or inventing a motive. |

## PEEL as a reasoning structure

The Point is an evaluative claim. Evidence is the source material that supports it. Explanation shows why that material warrants the claim. The Link connects the result to the task or thesis. These functions need not occupy four separate sentences or appear under visible labels.

Use a separate paragraph for each standard when the assignment requests that format. Otherwise organise paragraphs around the argument. Do not force all nine standards into a brief answer.

## Worked example

This example is synthetic. It supplies all the facts used below.

A student team's deck recommends buying an RFID tracking system for a laboratory. Slide 2 defines the goal as improving equipment availability. Slide 4 reports that 9 of 12 survey respondents found the paper sign out sheet frustrating. It does not measure whether equipment was available when needed.

> The survey information has limited relevance to the proposal's stated goal. Nine of twelve respondents found the sign out sheet frustrating, but the deck does not show that they were unable to obtain equipment. Dissatisfaction could concern the recording process even when enough equipment is available. The survey therefore supports investigating the sign out experience, while leaving the case for improved equipment availability unresolved. A measure of unmet equipment requests would connect the evidence more directly to the proposed purchase.

This tests the fit between evidence and purpose. It does not claim the survey result is inaccurate, that the team never gathered other evidence or that RFID cannot work.
