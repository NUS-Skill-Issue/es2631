# Evidence and accuracy

Trace factual support to material actually inspected. A deck is evidence of what its author reports. It is not automatic independent proof that every reported claim is true.

| Claim type | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Supplied source observation | Attribute it and retain a slide, page or passage location. |
| Retrieved external fact | Check the source's scope, date and measurement, then cite it. |
| Interpretation | Explain how it follows and mark uncertainty where the evidence leaves alternatives. |
| Derived number | Preserve inputs and arithmetic, and label the result as calculated. |
| Hypothetical example | Identify it as hypothetical. |
| Unsupported specific | Remove it, narrow the claim or identify the missing evidence. |

## Verification

Check each statistic, date, measurement, named finding, quotation, attribution and causal assertion against its source. Search for authoritative evidence when the task calls for current or externally verifiable facts. If access is unavailable, state the limit and continue at the supportable level.

For numerical claims, check value, sign, unit, denominator, baseline, population and period. A token such as “30” appearing elsewhere in a deck verifies none of those relationships. A correctly calculated percentage can still be irrelevant or misleading if its comparison is wrong.

Do not turn association into causation or a small trial into proof of broad effectiveness. Do not treat a source's use of “always” as sufficient grounds to repeat an unverified universal claim in your own voice.

Distinguish an absence of evidence in the provided material from evidence of absence in the world. Never invent personal experience, source details or the author's intent.

## References

Cite sources actually used, in the style the task requires. Do not invent a publisher, page number, DOI or quotation. Where the framework is discussed, use the edition supplied by the course or the public source actually inspected. See [course context](course-context.md).

In a finished draft, resolve unsupported specifics or explain the limitation outside the prose. Use visible placeholders only when the user wants a working draft with gaps. Never describe a draft containing unresolved placeholders as ready to submit.

The checker highlights a subset of claims for review. It may miss proper nouns, causal statements, unfamiliar number formats and implicit assumptions. Manual checking remains necessary even when it reports nothing.
