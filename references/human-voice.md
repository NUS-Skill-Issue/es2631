# Clear student writing

Preserve the user's meaning and level of confidence. Improve specificity, flow and readability without inventing experiences or making unsupported claims more certain.

Use concrete subjects and verbs. Replace vague praise with the observation that earns it. Explain what a limitation changes for the argument. Prefer familiar words when they carry the same meaning.

PEEL describes paragraph functions. Vary the sentence structure naturally while retaining an identifiable judgement, support and explanation. Do not insert vocabulary merely to satisfy the helper's pattern matching.

Avoid empty openings, repetitive transitions, inflated significance and vague attributions such as “studies show” without a source. Keep technical terms where they are needed. A word such as “robust” can have a legitimate technical meaning.

Follow the user's spelling, register and punctuation preferences. Do not universalise one student's preferences into a module rule. Preserve first person only where it fits the task and the underlying experience is supplied.

Style observations do not establish whether a text was written by AI. Do not promise that an edit will pass an AI detector. Preserve truthful AI use disclosures and never change “assisted” to “unassisted” because the wording now sounds natural.

## Final reading

Check that each paragraph advances the answer, that references support the exact claims and that the explanation is understandable without decoding a string of abstract nouns. Respect the stated maximum word count. Do not add a 10 percent allowance unless the brief permits it.
